## What is good (verified)

1. **No real money path.** The live loops and the dashboard only import the bridge's settings, sizing and data types. The MT5 broker code is reachable only from a manual command, and it refuses anything but a demo account.
2. **Both books add up.** Recomputed value matches; no repeated decisions; every stop and target is on the correct side; no open trade touched its stop or target without being closed; open trades have a recorded minute for every minute since entry (longest gap 1 minute).
3. **The original agent is untouched.** `TradingAgents` is a git checkout at the v0.7.1 tag (commit c454491, 27 Oct) with no local changes, and none of its files mentions our code. So "back up the original agent" is already true: git can restore it exactly.
4. **No secrets leaked.** Neither key appears anywhere outside `.env`, including the vault.
5. **Nothing is broken in the logs.** Both new loops have empty error logs. In two days there was one short TradingView slow-down.
6. **Tomorrow's launch is correct.** Dry run: Gemma gets one worker for the 10 pairs, Qwen gets one worker per pair, both write into `live_gemma` and `live_qwen`. The schedule says "not due" at 23:59 UTC and "due" at 00:31 UTC.
7. **The PC will not sleep** on mains power (High performance plan, sleep and hibernate set to never).
8. **Small footprint.** The whole project is 142 MB.

## Risks and gaps, most important first

1. **Our own code has no backup and no history.** The 10,905 lines of `paper_trade`, `tvmcp` and the monitor scripts are not in git. One bad edit or disk problem and there is nothing to go back to. (The original agent is safe; ours is not.)
2. **The agents think in days; the short-term account trades for 12 hours.** The agents reason from 50 and 200 day averages, weekly positioning, interest rates and growth numbers. Our exits are a stop about 0.17% away and a 12 hour limit. A correct view can be stopped out by noise. The retired daily accounts fitted the agents better.
3. **We throw away most of what the agents write.** Only Buy, Sell or Hold is used. We lose their timing ("wait for the news release"), their own stop and target, and "mild" (Qwen's 12 mild sells open the same trade as a full Sell).
4. **The $100 account has little room.** Open risk is $42.87 of a $47.58 limit (90%); gold alone is $24.91 of it. A second trade on any pair would be refused, so multiple trades per pair or hedging cannot work at this size without changing the limit or the account.
5. **The framework keeps one decision per pair per date.** Its memory log and its 5-day scoring are keyed by date. A second round the same day is skipped. More calls a day therefore need a separate run per time slot, and the agent's memory of past calls would be split between runs.
6. **Paper results are flattering for tight stops.** Stops fill at the exact price, with no spread or slippage, and gaps are modelled only at the open. The shorter the trade, the more this matters.
7. **Monitoring is tied to this chat session.** The 20-minute monitor job disappears when the session ends and expires on about 5 Nov. The trading loops are separate processes and keep running, but nothing restarts them after a reboot, and nothing alerts you if they die while this session is closed. The dashboard also needs to be started by hand: `python -m paper_trade.dashboard --run live_gemma_short --port 8765`.
8. **Tomorrow 00:30 UTC (07:30 your clock) is the first real use** of the new "each account makes its own calls" code. The dry run passed, but it has not run live yet.
9. **Retries are frequent.** The agents' structured answer fails and is retried as plain text about 4 times per Gemma decision (108 times in two days) and 3 to 4 times per Qwen pair-round. It works, but it costs extra calls, and one Qwen call (AUDUSD, 28 Oct) came out unreadable and was never traded. The reader was fixed for that shape.
10. **Test gaps.** There is no coverage tool installed (so no real percentage). Not tested end to end: the live loop for a whole day, the market feed layer, the phone-monitor script, the round-progress card against a round in progress, and all of the page code (checked by hand only).
11. **The phone monitor pages are out of date.** They do not show the entry, stop, target, win and lose amounts, orders, or round progress.
12. **The agents' history is short.** Our own daily record is 2 days old (the three stocks stopped on 28 Oct). The original agent was designed around months of daily history from its default data source.
13. **Qwen's free tokens last about 12 days** of daily rounds (6.3 M of 30 M already used; about 2.4 M a day). I do not know the price after that, or what happens when the free tokens run out.
14. **Still open from before:** the two API keys were pasted into chat and are not yet regenerated; the "market closed" price fix was never built (stocks are out; weekend forex is skipped, so it is not hurting now); gold at 0.01 lot moves the account $10 to $20 in an hour.

## Proposed plan (nothing started), updated with your priorities

Your priorities: (1) call the models often and make the frequency a setting per model, (2) use more of what the agents write, (3) mild calls trade smaller.

Rule for every phase: new behaviour is switched off by default, runs on a new account or in a read-only report, never changes the two running books, and ships with tests before it goes near live code.

**Phase 0: safety net (no behaviour change)**
- Put our code in git (leave out `runs/`, `.venv`, `.env`), tag today as the baseline, keep a zipped copy.
- Install a coverage tool (needs your OK). Write tests for the gaps in risk 10: a fake whole day through the loop, the monitor script, the round-progress card.

**Phase 1: scorecard (read-only)**
- Grade every call after 1 hour, 4 hours, 12 hours, 1 day and 3 days, ignoring our stops. Compare with TradingView's rating, "always sell" and a coin flip.
- It is also what later lets the call frequency follow model quality.

**Phase 2: calls as often as each model can manage (your priority)**
How it works, in plain words:
- Every model has its own schedule setting: "make a round of calls every N minutes". Your numbers as the starting point: Gemma (local) every 120 minutes, Qwen (online) every 60 minutes. A stronger, faster API such as OpenAI or Anthropic later is one more settings line with its own interval.
- Speed guard: a model never runs two rounds at once. If a round is still running when the next one is due, the next one waits until it finishes. Gemma's round takes about 90 minutes, so 120 fits with a 30 minute margin. Qwen's takes about 45, so 60 fits with a 15 minute margin.
- Each round gets its own time slot and its own folder of decisions, because the framework keeps only one decision per pair per date. The account and the dashboard read all slots in time order.
- When a new call arrives for a pair: Hold does nothing; the same direction as an open trade is ignored; the opposite direction closes the trade and opens the new one. (This needs no change to one-trade-per-pair.)
- Spend guard: a daily token limit per model, so a loop can never run up an unknown bill. When the limit is hit the loop stops starting rounds and the dashboard says so. You set the number.
- Later, the scorecard can raise or lower each model's frequency by how good its calls are. The first version is a plain setting.
- Open question: the agents' memory of past calls is split between slots. I would pass them the last few calls of each pair as text instead.
- Tests: a fake clock for the schedule (rounds start on time, never overlap, wait when the last one is late, stop at the limit), a dry run with fake workers, then a day on Gemma only (free) before Qwen.

**Phase 3: use more of what the agents write (your "yes")**
- Option per account: read the agent's entry condition, stop and target, and its strength, with our safety rules on top (a stop is always required, the risk limit still applies).
- Mild calls (Overweight, Underweight) trade at 75% size, as the old MT5 rules did.
- Tests: the reader is tried on the 40 saved reports before it goes live.

**Phase 4: exit what-if and the short-term agent variant**
- Replay the same calls with different exit styles on our recorded minute prices (reusing the live book's own exit code; a test must reproduce today's live trades exactly).
- The original agent stays untouched in git. Our layer adds a "short" version with a 12-hour rule and hourly and 4-hour candle tables from our own minute record.

**Phase 5: several trades per pair and hedging (last)**
- The book holds a list of trades per pair (about 6 files assume one). Needs the risk-limit question settled first.

Order: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Phase 2 starts with Gemma only.
