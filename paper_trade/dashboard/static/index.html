Decisions (what each model decided, per asset per date)

BUY opens a long trade (profits if the price rises). SELL opens a short trade (profits if the price falls). NO TRADE / KEEP OPEN is the model's Hold: it does nothing. It does not mean waiting to buy or sell. With no trade open nothing is opened; with a trade open it stays open until its stop-loss or take-profit is hit. "Mild" calls (Overweight, Underweight) count as BUY and SELL in the fixed-lot account. A faded BUY or SELL marked not opened was a real call that the account could not take: with a fixed 0.01 lot some trades risk more than the account's risk limit allows (gold and BTC always do on a $100 account). Orders and trades laid out like TradingView's trading panel, one row per trade

Every trade is opened by one order and closed by another, so a trade that has finished shows two orders. When a call flips (for example Buy to Sell), the Sell that closes the old long and the Sell that opens the new short happen together, so they look alike: "Opens #9" and "Closes #5" tell them apart. While a trade is open its row shows what it makes at its take profit and loses at its stop loss.

Paper money only: nothing here is connected to an exchange or broker. Fractional units, no leverage, 5 bps cost per trade, and short trades pay no borrow fee here (which flatters them a little). A trade wins when closing it made money after fees against the average entry price. A few weeks of decisions cannot show whether this system has an edge. Not financial advice.
